Nothing here is hosted for you. There's no account, no cloud, and no company holding your data. You run the server yourself, on a machine you control, and every device talks only to it. This website is a static page: it never sees your data or your setup codes.

Why

01

Your phone already has a screen time page. It tells you that you spent four hours on YouTube yesterday, and then lets you do it again.

Feeds are built by thousands of smart people whose job is to keep you scrolling. On the other side there's you: tired, at 1 a.m., deciding for the fortieth time whether to open one more video. It's not a fair fight, and willpower in the moment is the weakest tool you've got.

I've known people on all kinds of things. Up close, doomscrolling looks the same: the same chase, the same lying to yourself about how long it was, the same days and then years that just disappear. The difference is that it's legal, it's free, it's in everyone's pocket, and nobody is going to stop you.

Telemetry takes the decision out of the moment. You decide once, while you're calm. After that the system holds the line: feeds stay shut, making a rule looser waits until tomorrow, and the PIN can live with a friend.

It's built for people who know exactly what they want to do and still can't start it, or can't come back to it after a detour. Call it ADHD, burnout or just living in 2026. The tool is the same.

How it works

02

Collect

Small collectors on each device record what's in front of you: the app, the window title, the site, phone apps, sleep, steps, places. If the server is down they keep everything and catch up later.

Understand

Every 10 minutes an assistant reads the last two hours from all your devices and writes down what you're trying to do: which aims are active, which are waiting on an agent, and which you've dropped.

Hold the line

Distractions are closed by default. The blocking runs on each device, so it keeps working when the server or the internet doesn't.

Bring you back

When you drift, it notices whether and when you came back. Your assistant sees the same picture through MCP, so it can help you pick the thread back up instead of starting over.

Blocks

04

Closed by default

Feeds stay shut unless it's their time. You don't need to plan your day first for this to work.

Trimmed, not banned

While you work, YouTube keeps search and single videos and loses Shorts, the sidebar, comments and autoplay. Reddit opens single posts only.

Openings

Need something that's blocked? Ask. It makes you wait (ten minutes, say), then asks you to confirm again and again, moving the button each time. The urge is usually gone by the third.

Loosening waits a day

Any change that asks less of you starts tomorrow. Tightening a rule takes effect at once.

A PIN someone else holds

During the hours you choose, nothing can loosen the rules without a PIN, and you get three tries an hour. Your assistant can't lift it either.

Morning code & bedtime

The day stays shut until you scan a QR code you printed and left in the kitchen. Before bed the screens go grayscale, then only the essentials stay open.

No scolding

A blocked app is hidden with a small notice. A blocked page is covered where it is: your tabs are never closed or reloaded.

Understands

05

What you're doing, not just where

It groups apps, tabs, phone and agents into aims, like "editing the video" or "waiting on the meva agent", across all your devices. It never asks you for a plan.

Drift and return

It records stretches spent on distractions, what came just before them, and whether you got back to work within an hour. What it measures is the coming back, not the leaving.

Missing is missing

When a device sent nothing, the panel says so. It never counts that time as rest, focus or slacking.

Tells you when it breaks

If a collector stops while its computer is on, you get one message about it, not a stream of them.

Talks

06

An MCP server for your day

Any MCP client can read your day: Claude, Codex, ChatGPT, or a Telegram bot. It gets 38 tools, from activity_summary and drift_episodes to health_daily and what_owner_is_doing.

You approve every change

Your assistant can block something right away. It changes a label only after you approve the exact change in chat. Every change is logged, and undoing one takes a single call.

It can ask, never confirm

Your assistant can request an opening, but only you can confirm it.

Speaks up rarely

It messages you only when you wake up and after a long stretch off task.

Keeps

07

Nothing is deleted

Every observation is kept for good, along with its later corrections. Summaries are added next to the originals and never replace them.

Works offline

Each device saves to a local queue and catches up later. Blocking never waits for the server.

Updates itself

Releases are signed. Each update is checked after it installs and rolled back if it fails, and the database is backed up every night.

Setup about 15 minutes

10

You need one Linux machine that's always on (an old laptop, a home server or a cheap VPS) with Docker, and Tailscale (free) on every device.

  1. Let your agent do it

    Paste this into Claude Code, Codex, or any agent that runs commands. Before it installs anything, it stops, tells you what Telemetry will see and how to secure your machines, and offers to read the code first.

  2. Or do it by hand

    The server, on the always-on machine:

    $ curl -fsSL https://te.rime.cyou/i/server | bash

    $ curl -fsSL https://te.rime.cyou/i/mac | bash
    $ curl -fsSL https://te.rime.cyou/i/linux | bash

Questions

11
Isn't this just spyware?

Yes. It's spyware you install on yourself, on purpose. Nobody remembers losing three hours to YouTube: it doesn't feel like three hours while it's happening, and by evening it's gone. Something that watches everything and tells you plainly is the whole point.

The difference from the other kind is who it reports to: it runs on your machines and tells only you, plus the assistant you choose. It never reads what's on the pages you visit, but it does keep the text of your phone's notifications. That's why the server has to be yours, and locked down.

Do I need an AI assistant?

No. Collection, the panel and blocking all work without one. An assistant makes it much better, though: it's what turns a list of app names into "you were editing otuz.ecproj, then drifted to YouTube for 40 minutes". Any MCP client works, so you can use one you already pay for.

Why not just use Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing?

Those report what happened, and you can turn them off in two taps at exactly the wrong moment. Telemetry covers every device at once, makes looser rules wait a day, and lets someone else hold the key.

Can I cheat it?

Yes. They're your machines. The point isn't to make cheating impossible. It's to make it slow enough that the urge passes first, and it usually does.

Why does it need an always-on server?

Your phone and your computers need one place to meet, so the panel and your assistant see your whole day. The devices themselves don't depend on it: each one keeps collecting and blocking while the server is away, then catches up.

What does it run on?

macOS, Linux (window tracking needs KDE Plasma), Android, and Chromium-based browsers for the extension. The server runs anywhere Docker does. On a Mac, browsers without the extension, Safari included, are hidden while sites are blocked.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It's MIT licensed. Your costs are a machine to run it on and, if you want, an AI subscription.
